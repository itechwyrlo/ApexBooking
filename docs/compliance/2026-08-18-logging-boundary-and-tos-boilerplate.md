# Logging Boundary & ToS Boilerplate

Companion doc to [2026-08-18-payment-booking-security-hardening-design.md](../superpowers/specs/2026-08-18-payment-booking-security-hardening-design.md), Module 4. Documentation only — no code changes.

## Section A — Logging boundary

- **Never log the `Authorization` header**, in full or in part, in any log statement, exception message, or `ILogger` scope, anywhere in this codebase.
- **Never log a raw webhook request body.** `PayMongoWebhooksController.HandlePayMongoCallback` (see Module 3) now logs a SHA-256 hash of the body plus a fresh correlation id on failure, never the body itself.
- This app has **no request/response body-or-header-capturing logging middleware today** — confirmed: `Program.cs` registers no `UseHttpLogging()` or equivalent, and the only place a raw payload *was* being logged (the webhook controller's catch block) is fixed directly as part of Module 3. The correct guidance going forward is **"don't add one that captures bodies/headers unfiltered,"** not "add one with a redaction allowlist" — there's currently nothing to redact from because there's no blanket capture in the first place.
- **If body/header logging middleware is ever introduced later** (e.g. for request tracing), it must:
  - Set `HttpLoggingFields` to exclude `RequestHeaders`, `RequestBody`, and `ResponseBody` by default.
  - Explicitly strip `Authorization` from whatever header set remains enabled, via `HttpLoggingOptions.RequestHeaders.Remove("Authorization")` (or the equivalent for whichever logging middleware is chosen).
- **`GlobalExceptionHandler` spot-check** (`src/backend-api/ApexBooking.WebApi/Infrastructure/GlobalExceptionHandler.cs`): reviewed as part of this task. It logs `exception` + `exception.Message` only — it does not echo request bodies, headers, or any raw payload into its problem-details response or its log statement. `BusinessRuleBrokenException.Message` is returned to the client unconditionally (not gated behind `IsDevelopment()`, unlike the generic 500 case) — this is safe today because every `BusinessRuleBrokenException` thrown in this codebase, including the new ones added in Module 2/3, carries a static, human-authored message with no interpolated request content. **No fix needed** — flagging this as the boundary to preserve: any future `BusinessRuleBrokenException` message must stay free of raw user/payload data, since it is returned to the client as-is.

## Section B — ToS boilerplate

### 1. No Financial Custody / Technical Intermediary Status

`[Company Legal Name]` does not, at any point, take custody, possession, or control of any funds paid by a Customer to a Tenant. Each Tenant using the Service connects their own, independently-registered PayMongo merchant account, and all payment processing, fund settlement, and fund custody is performed exclusively by PayMongo (or the Tenant's chosen payment processor), pursuant to the Tenant's own agreement with that processor. `[Company Legal Name]` provides software that enables Tenants to configure their own payment processor credentials and to initiate payment requests through that processor's API on the Tenant's behalf; `[Company Legal Name]` acts solely as a technical intermediary transmitting data between the Tenant's customers and the Tenant's chosen payment processor, and is not a payment processor, money transmitter, or holder of funds in its own right.

### 2. Indemnification of Billing Disputes/Refunds

The Tenant is solely responsible for all billing disputes, chargebacks, refund requests, and related claims arising from transactions processed through the Tenant's connected payment processor account, including the Tenant's own pricing, cancellation policy, refund policy, and the quality or delivery of the Tenant's services. The Tenant agrees to indemnify, defend, and hold harmless `[Company Legal Name]`, its officers, employees, and affiliates from and against any and all claims, damages, losses, liabilities, costs, and expenses (including reasonable attorneys' fees) arising out of or relating to: (a) any billing dispute, chargeback, or refund claim brought by the Tenant's customers; (b) the Tenant's pricing, refund policy, or service delivery; and (c) the Tenant's use or misuse of its connected payment processor credentials through the Service.

### 3. DPA 2012 Data Role Splits (PIC vs PIP)

For purposes of the Philippines Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173) and its Implementing Rules and Regulations, each Tenant is the **Personal Information Controller (PIC)** with respect to the personal data of its own customers (including names, contact details, booking history, and payment-adjacent metadata collected through the Service). `[Company Legal Name]` acts as a **Personal Information Processor (PIP)**, processing such personal data solely on the Tenant's documented instructions and solely to provide the booking/scheduling functionality of the Service — `[Company Legal Name]` does not use, sell, or repurpose Tenant customer data for its own independent purposes. As PIP, `[Company Legal Name]`'s obligations include: implementing reasonable and appropriate organizational, physical, and technical security measures (see Section A above and the credential-encryption measures described in the accompanying design spec); notifying the Tenant without undue delay upon becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting the Tenant's customer data; and, upon termination of the Tenant's account, deleting or returning Tenant customer data in accordance with the Tenant's instructions and applicable law. Each Tenant, as PIC, remains responsible for its own registration and compliance obligations to the National Privacy Commission with respect to its processing of its customers' personal data.
